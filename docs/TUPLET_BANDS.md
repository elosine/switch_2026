## The rule, in four lines

- **A time container names a band** — slow · medium · fast — in STRIKES PER SECOND. Inside it, a player's single strikes of the phase form become tuplets.
- **The player's beat is the gap to its next strike** (the phase form's pulse at that moment: 54 … 133 bpm, drifting and gliding). The tuplet divides that gap.
- **The step chosen is the one of the ladder whose rate (strikes per beat × pulse ÷ 60) lands inside the band**, nearest the band's middle; if none lands inside at that pulse, the nearest step to the band.
- **Never above 10 strikes a second** (his ceiling, 2026-11-14).

## How a container is applied (for the AI that builds the pass)

1. The containers are a list he gives: start (score s) · length (s) · band — e.g. `1 s slow · 3 s medium · 2 s fast`, in whatever order he says; they tile a stretch of 277.8 … 480.
2. For every phase-form note (`properties.phaseForm.player · hit`) whose time falls in a container: its beat = the time to that player's next strike; its pulse = 60 ÷ beat; the step = this chart's pick for the container's band; the note becomes `step` strikes spread evenly over the beat (a `k in 2 beats` step spans two gaps: the pair of strikes becomes k even strikes).
3. The share of strikes that become tuplets may grow through a container or a run of them (SW-44: "3 single strikes and a tuplet, then the singles diminish") — the share is a second dial, not the band's.
4. During a glide the pulse moves strike by strike, so the pick may change inside one container — that is the acceleration he described, not a fault.
5. `node tools/tuplet_bands.js --pick <bpm> <band>` gives one answer with the alternatives that also land inside.
